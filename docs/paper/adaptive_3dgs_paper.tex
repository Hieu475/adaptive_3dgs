\begin{abstract}
Dense online 3D scene reconstruction from streaming RGB-D sensors requires maintaining visual and geometric fidelity under rigid per-frame execution deadlines. While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables real-time differentiable rendering, existing SLAM and mapping systems optimize Gaussians either indiscriminately or via heuristic residual thresholds (e.g., ``high rendering error $\Rightarrow$ optimize''). In this work, we demonstrate that high residual error does not imply high marginal optimization utility: on geometric boundaries, planar regions, and saturated surfaces, isolated gradient updates frequently degrade reconstruction quality ($U_i^\star < 0$). We formally re-frame online 3DGS scheduling as \textbf{budget-constrained marginal utility optimization}:
\begin{equation}
\max_{S_t \subseteq G_t} \Delta Q(S_t) \quad \text{subject to} \quad C(S_t) \le B_t
\end{equation}
where $U_i^\star = \Delta Q_i / C_i \in \mathbb{R}$ represents the counterfactual quality gain per unit of execution time. Across five protocol seeds ($n=5$), we show that: (1) optimization headroom is strictly positive ($H = +0.000149$), with $20.5\%$ of interventions inducing negative utility; (2) concurrent optimization exhibits severe sub-additivity ($R_{add} \to 0.0048$ at $|S|=16$) driven by alpha-compositing overlap ($\rho(\text{IoU}, |I|) = 0.5357, p = 0.0048$); and (3) a learned Two-Head MLP achieves $+108.0\%$ selection efficiency gains at tight compute budgets ($B \le 20\%$). Furthermore, investigating contextual conditioning $U^*(i \mid S)$ reveals a fundamental property: while co-visibility interactions modulate utility magnitude, within-frame candidate priority rank is substantially stable ($\bar{\rho}_{\text{rank}} = \mathbf{0.8916} \pm \mathbf{0.1104}$, Kendall $\bar{\tau} = \mathbf{0.8043}$, Top-5 overlap = $\mathbf{80.0\%}$ across 27 full-coverage groups). Consequently, adaptive contextual re-ranking introduces a $420\times$ selection overhead without statistically significant quality gains ($p > 0.70$), establishing that static pointwise utility already captures optimal prioritization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Real-time dense 3D reconstruction from streaming sensor observations is a core requirement in robotics, autonomous navigation, and spatial computing. Recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)~\cite{kerbl3Dgaussians} has emerged as a premier representation, delivering interactive novel-view synthesis through tile-based differentiable rasterization. In online SLAM settings (e.g., SplaTAM~\cite{keetha2023splatam}, RTG-SLAM~\cite{peng2024rtgslam}), thousands of Gaussians are continuously densified to represent newly observed geometry. However, optimizing all active Gaussians at every frame rapidly violates hard real-time latency deadlines ($15$--$33\text{ ms}$).

To operate within compute boundaries, existing systems adopt heuristic selection rules, most commonly prioritizing Gaussians with the largest photometric or geometric residuals~\cite{peng2024rtgslam}. We identify three critical limitations in this approach:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Residual Error $\neq$ Marginal Utility:} High rendering residual does not imply that optimizing a Gaussian will decrease global error. On occluding silhouettes and textured planes, single-step Adam updates frequently induce local tearing ($U_i^\star < 0$).
    \item \textbf{Severe Sub-Additivity:} Concurrent optimization of overlapping Gaussians suffers from severe alpha-compositing saturation: joint quality gain $\Delta Q(S)$ is vastly smaller than the sum of individual gains $\sum_{i \in S} \Delta Q_i$.
    \item \textbf{Orchestration Overhead:} In adaptive pipelines, the cost of extracting features and re-ranking candidates can exceed neural inference by orders of magnitude.
\end{enumerate}

In this work, we present a principled study of marginal utility estimation for online 3DGS under rigorous compute budgets. We design an isolated counterfactual intervention engine to supervise a Two-Head MLP, benchmark budget-constrained knapsack selection across five frozen seeds, and empirically characterize the impact of contextual interactions.

\section{Background and Related Work}

\subsection{3D Gaussian Splatting}
3DGS represents a continuous scene as an unstructured collection of anisotropic 3D Gaussians $G = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$. Each primitive $g_i$ is parameterized by its mean position $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance matrix $\Sigma_i \in \mathcal{S}_+^3$, opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$, and spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients $c_i$. Covariance is factorized as $\Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T$ using unit quaternion rotation $R_i$ and scaling vector $S_i$. Given camera pose $T = [W \mid t]$, the 3D covariance is projected into screen-space 2D covariance $\Sigma'_i = J W \Sigma_i W^T J^T$, where $J$ is the projective transformation Jacobian.

Pixel radiance $C_p$ along ray $p$ is computed via depth-sorted front-to-back alpha blending:
\begin{equation}
C_p = \sum_{i=1}^N c_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)
\label{eq:blending}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Online RGB-D Reconstruction and SLAM}
While offline 3DGS optimizes all Gaussians over tens of thousands of iterations, online RGB-D SLAM (e.g., SplaTAM~\cite{keetha2023splatam}, MonoGS~\cite{matsuki2024gaussian}, RTG-SLAM~\cite{peng2024rtgslam}) must track camera ego-motion and densify the map in a single pass. RTG-SLAM uses depth-guided keyframe selection and heuristic error thresholding. However, existing schedulers treat all visible primitives equally or rank them solely by static residual magnitude, failing to account for compute expenditure or optimization degradations.

\subsection{Compute-Aware Optimization}
In edge robotics and XR headsets, GPU execution deadlines are non-negotiable. Allocating compute based on marginal return is foundational in submodular optimization and knapsack scheduling. In this work, we integrate causal marginal utility estimation with differentiable volume rendering under explicit latency constraints.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Given an active 3D Gaussian map $G_t = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$ at frame $t$ and an optimization budget $B_t$ (e.g., $15\text{ ms}$), our objective is to select a subset $S_t \subseteq G_t$ that maximizes realized reconstruction quality gain under execution deadline constraints:
\begin{equation}
\max_{S_t \subseteq G_t} \Delta Q(S_t) \quad \text{subject to} \quad C(S_t) \le B_t
\end{equation}
The ground-truth marginal utility of candidate Gaussian $g_i$ is defined via isolated counterfactual intervention:
\begin{equation}
U_i^\star = \frac{\Delta Q_i}{C_i} = \frac{Q(G_t \cup \{\Delta \theta_i\}) - Q(G_t)}{\Delta T_i + \epsilon} \in \mathbb{R}
\end{equation}
where $Q$ balances photometric PSNR and geometric depth fidelity, and $C_i = \Delta T_i$ is the empirical kernel latency. Crucially, when an Adam step perturbs surface normals or introduces artifacts, $U_i^\star < 0$. Negative utility provides an indispensable penalization signal that prevents schedulers from wasting compute on converged or volatile primitives.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Gaussian State Representation \& Features}
To predict marginal utility prior to executing gradient updates, we extract an 11-dimensional observable state vector $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^{11}$ for each visible Gaussian $g_i$ (Figure~\ref{fig:state}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Photometric Residual ($e_{\text{rgb}}$)}: Mean absolute color error across the projected footprint.
    \item \textbf{Depth Residual ($e_{\text{depth}}$)}: Absolute L1 difference against sensor depth.
    \item \textbf{Gradient Norm ($\|\nabla_\mu \mathcal{L}\|_2$)}: Positional gradient magnitude from the current frame pass.
    \item \textbf{Attribution Mass ($m_{\text{attr}}$)}: Integrated rendering contribution $\sum_{p} \alpha_i T_{i,p}$.
    \item \textbf{Projected Area ($A_{\text{proj}}$)}: Screen-space bounding ellipse area in pixels.
    \item \textbf{Surface Uncertainty ($\sigma^2_{\text{depth}}$)}: Local geometric variance across spatial neighbors.
    \item \textbf{Positional Drift EMA ($d_{\text{pos}}$)}: Exponential moving average of spatial center displacement.
    \item \textbf{Residual Drift EMA ($d_{\text{res}}$)}: Temporal change in rendering error.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Frame Drift ($\Delta t_{\text{frame}}$)}: Number of frames elapsed since last observation.
    \item \textbf{Visibility Count ($v_{\text{count}}$)}: Cumulative number of frames where $g_i$ was visible.
    \item \textbf{Primitive Age ($\text{age}$)}: Frame count since initialization.
\end{enumerate}
To guarantee zero future data leakage, all features are normalized using parameters fitted strictly on training sequence frames (\texttt{train\_only}).

\subsection{Decoupled Two-Head Utility Predictor}
Instead of directly regressing scalar utility, our model decouples expected reconstruction gain $\widehat{\Delta Q}_i$ and execution cost $\widehat{C}_i$:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta Q}_i = f_Q(s_i; \theta_Q), \quad \widehat{C}_i = \text{Softplus}(f_C(s_i; \theta_C)), \quad \hat{U}_i = \frac{\widehat{\Delta Q}_i}{\widehat{C}_i + \epsilon}
\end{equation}
Cost positivity is enforced by design via Softplus activation. The network is optimized via difference-weighted pairwise ranking loss combined with scale-anchoring MSE:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{rank}} = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{(i,j) \in P} w_{ij} \log\left(1 + e^{-(\hat{U}_i - \hat{U}_j)}\right) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}
\end{equation}
where $P = \{(i,j) \mid U_i^\star > U_j^\star\}$ and $w_{ij} = |U_i^\star - U_j^\star| / \bar{w}$ prioritizes large utility margins.

\subsection{Budget-Constrained Subset Selection}
At frame $t$, the scheduler receives candidate pool $P_t$, predicted utilities $\hat{U}_i$, and modeled costs $\widehat{C}_i$. First, non-promising candidates are filtered:
\begin{equation}
P_t^+ = \{g_i \in P_t \mid \hat{U}_i > 0\}
\end{equation}
The knapsack optimizer then sorts $P_t^+$ in descending order of $\hat{U}_i$ and greedily selects subset $S_B = \{g_{\pi(1)}, \dots, g_{\pi(K)}\}$ such that $\sum_{k=1}^K \widehat{C}_{\pi(k)} \le B_t$. SelectiveAdam subsequently updates only parameters of $S_B$, while \texttt{FrozenBackgroundCache} preserves inactive primitives without recompilation.

\subsection{Context-Aware Conditional Utility (Phase 6)}
To investigate higher-order interactions, we formulate conditional marginal utility $U^*(i \mid S_t)$, representing the marginal gain of candidate $i$ given that subset $S_t$ has already been selected. The Residual Context Model predicts:
\begin{equation}
\hat{U}(i \mid S_t) = \hat{U}_{P4}(s_i) + \hat{R}(s_i, \mathcal{N}_i, \mathcal{O}_i, S_t)
\end{equation}
where $\hat{U}_{P4}$ is the strictly frozen Phase 4 backbone, $\mathcal{N}_i$ is the spatial KNN neighborhood (8D), $\mathcal{O}_i$ is screen-space IoU overlap (5D), and $S_t$ is the dynamic selected-set context (8D). Training enforces exact context group isolation $g = (\text{scene}, \text{frame}, \text{sorted}(S_t))$ via \texttt{GroupedBatchSampler}.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
All experiments follow Confirmatory Protocol v1 across five frozen random seeds ($n=5$: \texttt{[42, 43, 44, 45, 46]}). Evaluations use real RGB-D sequences from the TUM benchmark:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Split}: \texttt{tum\_fr1\_desk} (frames 0–40, $N=640$ interventions).
    \item \textbf{Validation Split}: \texttt{tum\_fr1\_desk} (frames 41–60, $N=240$).
    \item \textbf{Zero-Shot Cross-Scene Test Split}: \texttt{tum\_fr2\_xyz} ($N=240$ evaluated on unseen scene and camera dynamics).
\end{itemize}
We report $95\%$ bootstrap confidence intervals across seeds. Candidate pool coverage is audited at $100\%$ ($|M_t| = |P_t| = 20$), and synthetic baseline fill is strictly prohibited ($0\%$).

\section{Experimental Results}

\subsection{Oracle Measurability, Headroom \& Non-Additivity}
Evaluating $704$ counterfactual interventions demonstrates that marginal utility is well-defined and measurable ($\text{Var}(U^\star) > 0$). Optimization headroom $H = \Delta Q(S_K^\star) - \Delta Q(S_{\text{random}}) = \mathbf{+0.000149} > 0$ confirms substantial potential for selective scheduling. Crucially, **$20.5\%$** of all valid single-Gaussian interventions degraded reconstruction quality ($U_i^\star < 0$, Figure~\ref{fig:dist}), disproving the common heuristic assumption that optimizing high-error regions is universally beneficial.

Furthermore, direct measurement of joint group additivity $R_{add}(S) = \frac{\Delta Q(S)}{\sum_{i \in S} \Delta Q_i}$ reveals acute sub-additivity:
\begin{equation}
R_{add}(|S|=4) = \mathbf{0.2249} \ll 1.0, \quad R_{add}(|S|=16) = \mathbf{0.0048} \ll 1.0
\end{equation}
Concurrent optimization saturates ray transmittance, diminishing marginal returns as group cardinality expands.

\subsection{Utility Prediction Fidelity \& Feature Ladder}
Table~\ref{tab:p4_benchmark} compares TwoHeadMLP against heuristic baselines under zero-shot transfer to \texttt{tum\_fr2\_xyz}. While Error $\times$ Influence scores high rank correlation with unnormalized loss, it yields poor physical scale calibration (MAE = $2.30$). In contrast, TwoHeadMLP achieves the lowest physical error ($\text{MAE} = 2.77 \times 10^{-3}$) and superior selection efficiency ($\text{OSE@20} = 0.497 \pm 0.102$, a $+108.0\%$ relative gain over RGB Error, Figure~\ref{fig:pred}).

Ablating feature subsets (V0 appearance $\to$ V7 full observable state) demonstrates that incorporating depth uncertainty and temporal drift EMAs improves NDCG@20\% by $+18.4\%$ relative to appearance-only models.

\subsection{Budget-Constrained Selection Sweeps}
Figure~\ref{fig:budget} depicts realized quality gains across budget levels $B \in \{10\%, 20\%, 40\%, 60\%, 80\%\}$. At $B=20\%$, TwoHeadMLP achieves $\Delta Q = 4.08 \times 10^{-5}$ vs $1.96 \times 10^{-5}$ for Error-Only ($+108.0\%$ gain). At large budgets ($B \ge 60\%$), all policies converge toward the unconstrained upper bound, proving that intelligent utility estimation is most critical under strict compute starvation. In multi-frame online streaming, our scheduler cuts optimization latency by $33.6\%$ vs heuristic knapsack ($188.1\text{ ms}$ vs $283.2\text{ ms}$) and $47.7\%$ vs error-only ($359.8\text{ ms}$) while preserving peak PSNR ($13.07\text{ dB}$, $100\%$ frame win rate).

\subsection{Contextual Interactions and Case B Resolution}
Table~\ref{tab:p6_ablation} reports our systematic 8-variant ablation ladder. \texttt{self\_neighbor\_selected} (P6-F, 27D) emerges as the top-performing model ($\rho = 0.2353$, $\text{NDCG@5} = 0.7681$). Incorporating unconstrained screen-overlap in full 32D (\texttt{all\_features}) leads to overfitting and generalization loss ($\rho = 0.0780$).

Pairwise interaction measurements confirm that screen-space co-visibility drives sub-additivity ($\text{Spearman}(\text{IoU}, |I|) = \mathbf{0.5357}, p = \mathbf{0.0048}$; $100\%$ sub-additive for $\text{IoU} \ge 0.30$). However, evaluating full candidate pools across 27 exact context groups (with zero synthetic defaulting) reveals an unexpected phenomenon:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Mean Spearman rank correlation: $\bar{\rho}_{\text{rank}} = \mathbf{0.8916} \pm \mathbf{0.1104}$ (median $\rho = 0.9188$).
    \item Mean Kendall rank correlation: $\bar{\tau} = \mathbf{0.8043}$.
    \item Top-5 candidate overlap: $\mathbf{80.0\%}$ (Top-10 overlap: $\mathbf{89.3\%}$, Top-3 overlap: $\mathbf{72.8\%}$).
\end{itemize}

As visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:context_paired} and Figure~\ref{fig:stability}, co-visibility attenuates candidate utility monotonically through rasterization saturation, but preserves relative ranking. In the 5-Policy Oracle Decomposition, Oracle Conditional achieves identical quality to Oracle Static (Context Advantage $\equiv +0.00 \times 10^{-5}$ at $30\%$ and $60\%$ budgets). In online selection benchmarks across five seeds, $Q(P_6) - Q(P_4)$ shows no statistically significant improvement ($p > 0.70$, 95\% bootstrap CI spans zero).

\section{AI Systems Analysis \& Overhead}
Figure~\ref{fig:latency} details the stage-by-stage execution latency under $B_{\text{sched}} = 15.0\text{ ms}$. Total pipeline stage latency is $T_{\text{total}} = 924.59\text{ ms}$, partitioned into:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Feature Extraction: $T_{\text{feat}} = 225.79\text{ ms}$ (24.4\%)
    \item Context Construction: $T_{\text{ctx}} = 66.84\text{ ms}$ (7.2\%)
    \item Model Forward Pass: $T_{\text{MLP}} = \mathbf{1.12\text{ ms}}$ ($\mathbf{0.1\%}$)
    \item Subset Selection: $T_{\text{sel}} = 76.36\text{ ms}$ (8.3\%)
    \item Gaussian Optimization: $T_{\text{opt}} = 554.48\text{ ms}$ (60.0\%)
\end{itemize}

This profile surfaces an essential AI Systems insight: \textbf{neural inference itself is cheap ($1.12\text{ ms}$); state extraction, spatial indexing, and selection orchestration dominate adaptive overhead.} Adaptive greedy re-ranking incurs a $420\times$ selection latency penalty over static sorting ($76.36\text{ ms}$ vs $0.18\text{ ms}$). Given that Case B demonstrates $\operatorname{rank}(U^*(i \mid S)) \approx \operatorname{rank}(U^*(i \mid \emptyset))$, paying this runtime penalty yields zero realized quality advantage in online SLAM.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Short Horizon}: Oracle utilities are evaluated over $M=5$ gradient steps; longer multi-frame trajectory coupling remains unmodeled.
    \item \textbf{Non-Additivity}: While sub-additivity is proven, knapsack selection relies on a linear additive relaxation.
    \item \textbf{Dataset Scope}: Confirmatory experiments focus on TUM RGB-D sequences; outdoor unbounded transfer requires further evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Systems Bottlenecks}: CPU-GPU serialization and Python dispatch currently dominate runtime, highlighting the necessity of fully fused CUDA kernels.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
We have presented a causal marginal utility estimation framework for online RGB-D 3D Gaussian Splatting under compute budgets. By proving that high rendering error does not imply optimization utility, we established that $20.5\%$ of interventions degrade reconstruction fidelity. Our learned Two-Head MLP achieves $+108.0\%$ selection efficiency gains under tight compute budgets. Furthermore, rigorous contextual characterization resolves Case B: co-visibility modulates utility magnitude while candidate priority ranks remain substantially stable ($\bar{\rho} = 0.8916$, Overlap@5 = $80.0\%$), rendering costly adaptive re-ranking unnecessary under the evaluated budget regime.
