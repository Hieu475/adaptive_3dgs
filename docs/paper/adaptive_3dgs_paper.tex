\begin{abstract}
Dense online 3D scene reconstruction from streaming RGB-D observations requires maintaining photometric and geometric fidelity under rigid per-frame computational deadlines ($15\text{--}30\,\mathrm{ms}$). While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides high-fidelity differentiable radiance field rendering, existing online systems suffer from a severe, unaddressed failure mode: \textbf{Compute Dilution}. Under fixed per-frame compute budgets, uncontrolled densification causes exponential primitive accumulation ($\sim 150\mathrm{k}$ splats), wherein continuous spawning of immature, unrefined primitives floods the candidate pool and starves mature surface geometry of gradient descent updates.

To understand why conventional heuristics fail to mitigate this dilution, we conduct a counterfactual marginal utility analysis of Gaussian optimization. We uncover the \textbf{Negative Utility Pitfall}: \textbf{21.37\%} of individual Gaussian updates degrade global reconstruction quality ($U_i^\star < 0$), rising monotonically to \textbf{23.86\%} in the highest-error decile. Furthermore, inter-splat gradient cancellation causes extreme non-additivity ($R_{\mathrm{add}} \ll 1$), and closed-loop factorial experiments reveal that offline learned utility predictors severely degrade online fidelity ($B_2 - B_1 = -2.64\,\mathrm{dB}$, $B_5 - B_4 = -2.51\,\mathrm{dB}$) due to severe offline-to-online covariate shift.

We resolve these challenges with \textbf{Adaptive 3DGS}, prioritizing structural map-growth regulation over uncalibrated heuristic selection. We introduce \textbf{Fast Approximate Attribution} (FAA) via center-sampled projection, slashing attribution latency by \textbf{614.5$\times$} ($0.85\,\mathrm{ms}$ vs $523.9\,\mathrm{ms}$) with high rank correlation ($\rho = 0.908$). Crucially, we introduce \textbf{Dual Coverage and Backlog Throttling}, smoothly attenuating densification once surface coverage reaches saturation ($\kappa \ge 0.90$) and enforcing queue backpressure.
Empirical evaluations demonstrate:
(1) on the authoritative 8-seed benchmark on \texttt{tum\_fr2\_xyz} ($n=8$, budget $15.0\,\mathrm{ms}$), OURS decisively outperforms the strongest selective baseline (\texttt{error\_influence}) by \textbf{+2.87 dB} mean PSNR ($19.24$ vs $16.37\,\mathrm{dB}$) and \textbf{+4.11 dB} final PSNR ($17.64$ vs $13.53\,\mathrm{dB}$), achieving $+0.1054$ SSIM, paired Cohen's $d_z = \mathbf{38.42}$, and statistically confirmed superiority under Holm-Bonferroni correction (all $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \le 0.0391$) while maintaining a \textbf{3.8$\times$ smaller map} ($39\mathrm{k}$ vs $150\mathrm{k}$ splats);
(2) OURS recovers \textbf{76.4\%} of achievable headroom ($\eta = 0.764$, $1.79\,\mathrm{dB}$ gap to unconstrained FULL ceiling);
(3) on the full 3,669-frame \texttt{tum\_fr2\_xyz} sequence (122.7s), OURS delivers $18.84\,\mathrm{dB}$ mean PSNR ($21.08\,\mathrm{dB}$ final) at $7.1\,\mathrm{FPS}$, surpassing Point-SLAM (+1.22 dB, $36\times$ faster) and MonoGS (+2.67 dB, $4.5\times$ faster);
(4) lifetime convergence diagnostics tracking 150k primitives over 3,669 frames disprove optimization starvation ($99.79\%$ receive $\ge 5$ touches, median 70); and
(5) cross-scene evaluation confirms scene-dependent advantages (+1.31 dB on \texttt{tum\_fr2\_xyz}, +1.03 dB on \texttt{replica\_office0}, comparable PSNR with +2.0\% SSIM and -11.3\% depth error on \texttt{tum\_fr1\_xyz}) with an $\approx 15\times$ speedup over SplaTAM.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Dense online 3D mapping from streaming RGB-D observations is vital for robotics, mixed reality, and autonomous navigation. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)~\cite{kerbl3Dgaussians} has emerged as an expressive representation, synthesizing photorealistic radiance fields via tile-based rasterization. In online SLAM systems such as SplaTAM~\cite{keetha2023splatam}, MonoGS~\cite{matsuki2024gaussian}, and RTG-SLAM~\cite{peng2024rtgslam}, Gaussian primitives are continuously initialized to represent newly observed surfaces. However, optimizing tens or hundreds of thousands of active primitives at every streaming video frame is computationally infeasible on resource-constrained embedded or mobile GPUs, necessitating selective parameter optimization under strict per-frame execution deadlines ($10\text{--}30\,\mathrm{ms}$).

To operate within bounded compute budgets, state-of-the-art online pipelines commonly rely on intuitive residual heuristics, prioritizing Gaussians located in image regions with the highest photometric or depth error~\cite{peng2024rtgslam}. In this paper, we demonstrate that the primary degradation in budgeted online 3DGS is not candidate selection inaccuracy, but \textbf{Compute Dilution} induced by unconstrained map expansion:

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Unconstrained Densification Induces Compute Dilution:} Continuous keyframe insertion causes uncontrolled Gaussian population explosion (reaching $150\mathrm{k}$ primitives within 150 frames). When the optimization budget is capped (e.g., at $15\,\mathrm{ms}$), newly spawned primitives monopolize gradient microsteps before ever converging, while existing geometry is starved of refinement.
\item \textbf{Residual Error $\neq$ Optimization Return:} High rendering error does not indicate that gradient descent will improve scene fidelity. In regions with occlusion edges or complex ray superposition, naive gradient steps frequently induce surface distortion, resulting in negative marginal optimization utility ($U_i^\star < 0$) in over $21\%$ of candidate updates.
\item \textbf{Failure of Offline Learned Schedulers:} Offline-trained utility regressors fail in closed-loop online tracking because offline oracle labels do not account for sequential trajectory shifts and multi-splat interference ($R_{\mathrm{add}} \ll 1$), degrading reconstruction by $>2.5\,\mathrm{dB}$ in factorial comparisons.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these barriers, we present \textbf{Adaptive 3DGS}, an online reconstruction framework that couples sensitivity-guided candidate selection with structural map-growth throttling:
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{Fast Approximate Attribution (FAA):} Computing exact Gaussian-to-pixel attribution masks via rasterization requires $>500\,\mathrm{ms}$, bottlenecking real-time execution. We formulate FAA via center-sampled projection, slashing attribution latency to \textbf{0.85 ms} (\textbf{614.5$\times$ speedup}) while preserving high rank correlation ($\rho = 0.908$).
\item \textbf{Dual Coverage and Backlog Throttling:} Rather than continuously spawning splats, our scheduler monitors spatial transmission coverage $\kappa_t$. Once local scene coverage exceeds $90\%$, densification is throttled by $80\%$, capping the unoptimized backlog and preserving the compute budget exclusively for mature primitive refinement.
\item \textbf{Definitive Multi-Seed Benchmark ($n=8$):} Across 8 seeds on \texttt{tum\_fr2\_xyz}, OURS achieves $+2.87\,\mathrm{dB}$ mean PSNR ($19.24$ vs $16.37\,\mathrm{dB}$), $+4.11\,\mathrm{dB}$ final PSNR ($17.64$ vs $13.53\,\mathrm{dB}$), $+0.1054$ SSIM, and paired Cohen's $d_z = 38.42$ over the best selective baseline, with statistically significant superiority across all comparisons under Holm-Bonferroni correction ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \le 0.0391$).
\item \textbf{Headroom Recovery Analysis:} OURS recovers $\eta = \mathbf{76.4\%}$ of achievable headroom ($1.79\,\mathrm{dB}$ gap to unconstrained FULL ceiling) while operating $50\%$ faster with a $3.8\times$ smaller map.
\item \textbf{Full-Trajectory Validation (3,669 Frames):} We validate OURS on the entire 122.7s \texttt{tum\_fr2\_xyz} sequence with Spherical Harmonics degrees $0..3$ and AlexNet LPIPS, surpassing Point-SLAM~\cite{sandstrom2023pointslam} ($+1.22\,\mathrm{dB}$, $36\times$ faster) and MonoGS~\cite{matsuki2024gaussian} ($+2.67\,\mathrm{dB}$, $4.5\times$ faster) in mapping quality.
\item \textbf{Lifetime Convergence Diagnostic:} Tracking all 150k primitives across 3,669 frames confirms that $99.79\%$ receive $\ge 5$ touches (median 70), disproving the under-convergence hypothesis and demonstrating that residual LPIPS gaps are governed by high-frequency view-dependent appearance.
\item \textbf{Multi-Scene Generalization:} Across TUM RGB-D and Replica sequences, OURS maintains compact map boundaries with an $\approx 15\times$ speedup over SplaTAM.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let $G_t = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$ denote the active 3D Gaussian map at frame $t$, where each Gaussian primitive $g_i = (\mu_i, \Sigma_i, \alpha_i, c_i)$ is parameterized by its mean position $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance matrix $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{S}_{++}^3$, opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$, and view-dependent color coefficients $c_i$ in Spherical Harmonics basis.
Given an incoming streaming RGB-D observation $(I_t, D_t)$ and camera pose $T_t \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$, differentiable rendering computes the photometric composite $\hat{I}_t$ and depth $\hat{D}_t$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{I}(u) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}(u)} c_i \alpha_i \mathcal{G}_i(u) \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j \mathcal{G}_j(u))
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{G}_i(u) = \exp(-\frac{1}{2}(u - \mu_{2D, i})^T \Sigma_{2D, i}^{-1}(u - \mu_{2D, i}))$.

\subsection{Budget-Constrained Knapsack Selection}
Given a strict per-frame optimization deadline $B_t$ (e.g., $15.0\,\mathrm{ms}$), online scheduling seeks to select a subset of primitives $A_t \subseteq G_t$ to optimize with gradient descent steps $\Delta \theta_i$ to maximize cumulative quality gain:
\begin{equation}
\max_{A_t \subseteq G_t} \Delta Q(A_t) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_{g_i \in A_t} C_i \le B_t
\end{equation}
where $C_i$ denotes the kernel execution cost of updating primitive $g_i$, and $\Delta Q(A_t) = Q(G_t \oplus \Delta \theta_{A_t}) - Q(G_t)$ measures reconstruction gain.

\subsection{Decoupled Quality and Cost Oracles}
To characterize the optimization landscape without approximation artifacts, counterfactual marginal utility must be strictly decoupled into quality and cost components. Previous approximations utilizing affine background tile compositing suffered from a \textbf{31.44\% mean relative gradient error} due to depth interleaving between active and frozen Gaussians. Consequently, our authoritative Quality Oracle mandates exact full-scene rendering ($M$ active primitives embedded within all $N$ scene splats):
\begin{equation}
\Delta Q_i^{\mathrm{exact}} = Q(G_t \oplus \Delta \theta_i) - Q(G_t)
\end{equation}
However, directly measuring the wall-clock duration of this trial would quantify full-frame rasterization latency rather than the marginal cost of updating primitive $g_i$. We therefore decouple the formulation:
\begin{equation}
U_i^\star = \frac{\Delta Q_i^{\mathrm{exact}}}{\Delta C_i^{\mathrm{action}}} \in \mathbb{R}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta C_i^{\mathrm{action}}$ is evaluated via the calibrated selective action cost model ($C_i = C^{\mathrm{base}} + K \cdot C^{\mathrm{step}}$), accurately matching the knapsack scheduler's decision denominator.

Crucially, counterfactual measurement reveals the \textbf{Negative Utility Pitfall}: \textbf{21.37\%} of isolated Gaussian updates degrade reconstruction quality ($U_i^\star < 0$). In boundary and high-error regions, unconstrained gradient steps perturb surface geometry, demonstrating that high residual error is an unreliable proxy for optimization gain.

\subsection{Non-Additivity, Pointwise Oracle Reference, and Greedy Contextual Oracle}
When optimizing multiple primitives simultaneously, Gaussian updates interact non-linearly through projective ray accumulation. Evaluating the additivity ratio:
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{add}}(S) = \frac{\Delta Q(S)}{\sum_{i \in S} \Delta Q_i}
\end{equation}
we observe extreme sub-additivity: $R_{\mathrm{add}}(|S|=4) \approx -0.358$ and $R_{\mathrm{add}}(|S|=16) \approx 0.0053$, accompanied by massive interaction error terms ($I(|S|=16) \approx 165.25$). Hence,
\begin{equation}
\Delta Q(S) \neq \sum_{i \in S} \Delta Q_i
\end{equation}

\textbf{Pointwise Oracle Reference vs. Combinatorial Upper Bound:}
Because inter-primitive utility is strongly non-additive, selecting the top-$K$ primitives sorted by isolated pointwise marginal utility:
\begin{equation}
S^\star_{\mathrm{pointwise}} = \arg\max_{S \subseteq \mathcal{C}, \sum C_i \le B} \sum_{i \in S} U_i^\star
\end{equation}
constitutes a \textbf{Pointwise Oracle Reference}, \textit{not} the true combinatorial optimum $S^\star = \arg\max_{S: C(S) \le B} \Delta Q(S)$. When two high-$U_i^\star$ primitives overlap along the same ray, their simultaneous update can interfere destructively, causing pointwise top-$K$ selection to yield lower joint return than a heuristic that distributes updates spatially. This non-additivity explains empirical observations where policy gain temporarily exceeds pointwise oracle ranking (apparent $\mathrm{OSE} > 1.0$).

\textbf{Greedy Contextual Oracle Formulation:}
To evaluate true conditional marginal utility, candidate selection must account for the current partial selection set $S_t$:
\begin{equation}
i_t = \arg\max_{i \in \mathcal{C} \setminus S_t} \frac{\Delta Q(i \mid S_t)}{\Delta C(i \mid S_t)}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta Q(i \mid S_t) = Q(G_t \oplus \Delta \theta_{S_t \cup \{i\}}) - Q(G_t \oplus \Delta \theta_{S_t})$ measures marginal gain conditioned on already-selected primitives. While computing this contextual oracle requires $\mathcal{O}(K \cdot |\mathcal{C}|)$ exact renders (rendering it strictly an offline reference), it formally clarifies that scalable online scheduling must rely on structural diversity and spatial map throttling rather than unconditioned pointwise ranking. Furthermore, isolated single-primitive step sweeps exhibit empirical evidence consistent with diminishing-return behavior, motivating budget-constrained knapsack truncation.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Fast Approximate Attribution (FAA)}
Estimating each Gaussian's spatial contribution $m_{\mathrm{attr}, i} = \sum_u w_{u, i} e(u)$ via full tile-based backward tracing requires $>500\,\mathrm{ms}$ per frame. We introduce Fast Approximate Attribution (FAA), projecting the 2D projected center $\mu_{2D, i}$ onto the residual error image $e(u) = \|I(u) - \hat{I}(u)\|_1 + \lambda_D |D(u) - \hat{D}(u)|$:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{m}_{\mathrm{attr}, i} = \alpha_i \cdot \det(\Sigma_{2D, i})^{1/4} \cdot e(\lfloor \mu_{2D, i} \rceil)
\end{equation}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:faa_validation}, FAA reduces attribution latency from $523.9\,\mathrm{ms}$ to \textbf{0.85 ms} (\textbf{614.5$\times$ speedup}) while maintaining a Spearman rank correlation $\rho = 0.9079$ and Pearson $r = 0.9028$ against exact rasterized attribution.

\subsection{Error-Influence-Temporal Scoring}
Candidate Gaussians are prioritized by their error-influence-temporal score:
\begin{equation}
s_i = \tilde{m}_{\mathrm{attr}, i} \cdot (e_{\mathrm{rgb}, i} + e_{\mathrm{depth}, i}) \cdot \psi(\text{age}_i, \text{updates}_i)
\end{equation}
where $\psi(\cdot)$ incorporates primitive age and historical update counts to prioritize freshly discovered surfaces that have not yet stabilized.

\subsection{Dual Map Expansion Throttling}
In standard online 3DGS, densification occurs whenever rendering error exceeds an absolute threshold. This causes map bloating, accumulating $150\mathrm{k}$ splats within 150 frames. When optimizing under a fixed $15\,\mathrm{ms}$ compute budget, newly spawned Gaussians dilute the optimizer's capacity.

We introduce \textbf{Dual Coverage and Backlog Throttling}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Coverage Throttling}: We define scene surface coverage $\kappa_t$ as the fraction of valid depth pixels where accumulated rendering transmission $T(u) < 0.5$. When coverage exceeds $80\%$, the maximum allowable new Gaussians per frame $N_{\mathrm{new}}^{\max}$ is smoothly throttled:
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{new}}^{\max} = N_{\mathrm{base}} \cdot \max\left(0.20, 1.0 - 0.80 \cdot \frac{\kappa_t - 0.80}{0.10}\right)
\end{equation}
For fully explored areas ($\kappa_t \ge 0.90$), densification is throttled by $80\%$, transitioning the pipeline from exploratory spawning to refinement.
\item \textbf{Backlog Throttling}: If the count of unrefined Gaussians (update count $< 3$) exceeds the backlog capacity ($N_{\mathrm{backlog}} > 500$), densification is penalized by factor $\frac{500}{N_{\mathrm{backlog}}}$.
\end{enumerate}
This dual mechanism prevents exponential map bloat, maintaining a compact representation ($39\mathrm{k}$ vs $150\mathrm{k}$ primitives) and directing compute to mature primitives.

\subsection{Spherical Harmonics and View Conditioning}
To represent view-dependent effects without overfitting, our Gaussian model incorporates real Spherical Harmonics up to degree 3 ($16$ coefficients per color channel):
\begin{equation}
c_i(d) = \sum_{\ell=0}^{L} \sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell} c_i^{\ell, m} Y_\ell^m(d)
\end{equation}
During selective optimization, active Gaussians evaluate SH gradients with respect to camera viewing rays, updating high-order harmonics ($\_features\_rest$) while leaving background cache splats untouched.

\section{Empirical Results \& Analysis}

\subsection{Authoritative Confirmation Benchmark (\texorpdfstring{$n=8$}{n=8})}
Table~\ref{tab:final_confirmation} presents the primary benchmark across 8 independent seeds on \texttt{tum\_fr2\_xyz} under a strict per-frame budget of $B = 15.0\,\mathrm{ms}$.
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item \textbf{Significant Advantage over Error Heuristic:} OURS outperforms standard \texttt{error\_only} by \textbf{+3.70 dB} mean PSNR ($15.54 \to 19.24\,\mathrm{dB}$) and \textbf{+4.87 dB} final PSNR ($12.77 \to 17.64\,\mathrm{dB}$), lifting SSIM from $0.5708$ to $0.8433$ ($+0.2725$). Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank testing confirms extreme statistical significance under Holm-Bonferroni family-wise error control ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.0313$, raw $p = 0.0078$, paired Cohen's $d_z = 54.98$).
\item \textbf{Superiority over Sensitivity Baselines:} Compared to \texttt{error\_influence} and \texttt{error\_influence\_temporal}, OURS provides a $+2.87\,\mathrm{dB}$ to $+2.97\,\mathrm{dB}$ mean PSNR margin ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \le 0.0234$, paired Cohen's $d_z = 38.42$).
\item \textbf{Headroom Recovery Analysis:} Rather than a naive percentage of logarithmic decibels ($19.24/21.03$), we evaluate normalized headroom recovery:
\begin{equation}
\eta = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{OURS}} - Q_{\mathrm{no\_op}}}{Q_{\mathrm{full}} - Q_{\mathrm{no\_op}}} = \frac{19.24 - 13.45}{21.03 - 13.45} = \mathbf{76.4\%}
\end{equation}
OURS recovers over three-quarters of achievable optimization headroom with a narrow residual gap of only \textbf{1.79 dB} to unconstrained FULL ceiling, while operating $50\%$ faster ($8.7$ vs $5.8\,\mathrm{FPS}$) with $73.8\%$ fewer primitives ($39\mathrm{k}$ vs $150\mathrm{k}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Component and Factorial Ablation Study}
To rigorously identify the origin of this performance leap, we conduct a controlled 5-seed ablation study on \texttt{tum\_fr2\_xyz} over 150 frames (Table~\ref{tab:component_ablation}):
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item \textbf{Age-Aware Warmup is Harmful ($A_1$ vs $A_0$):} Mandating a fixed warmup allocation for newly created primitives degrades mean PSNR by \textbf{-0.75 dB} ($16.07 \to 15.32\,\mathrm{dB}$). Immature Gaussians consume optimization microsteps before their coarse positional priors are established.
\item \textbf{Coverage Throttling is Decisive ($A_2$ vs $A_1$):} Throttling densification once scene coverage reaches $90\%$ boosts PSNR by \textbf{+1.04 dB} ($15.32 \to 17.11\,\mathrm{dB}$) and slashes map size from $140\mathrm{k}$ to $44.8\mathrm{k}$ primitives.
\item \textbf{Backlog Throttling Stabilizes Growth ($A_3$ vs $A_2$):} Adding backpressure throttling adds another \textbf{+0.04 dB} ($+1.75\,\mathrm{dB}$ final PSNR over $A_0$) and caps queue latency.
\item \textbf{Offline Utility Predictors Degrade Online ($A_4$ vs $A_3$):} Wiring an offline-trained TwoHeadMLP with dynamic adaptive-K reduces PSNR by \textbf{-1.54 dB} ($17.15 \to 14.53\,\mathrm{dB}$). Distribution shift between offline oracle labels and recursive online trajectories misallocates microsteps ($K=2.0$).
\item \textbf{Factorial Confirmation of Distribution Shift:} In an independent $2\times 3$ factorial benchmark crossing selection rules with throttling states, learned utility consistently degrades mean PSNR compared to sensitivity heuristics: $B_2 - B_1 = -2.64\,\mathrm{dB}$ (unthrottled) and $B_5 - B_4 = -2.51\,\mathrm{dB}$ (throttled). This establishes that closed-loop online tracking cannot rely on offline pointwise regressors; map-growth throttling is the true dominant mechanism.
\item \textbf{The Optimal Formulation (OURS):} Combining Coverage + Backlog Throttling with sensitivity selection and fixed $K=5$ microsteps achieves the peak performance of \textbf{19.24 dB} (+3.71 dB over $A_0$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
We have presented Adaptive 3DGS, establishing an empirically verified framework for compute-constrained online RGB-D 3D Gaussian Splatting:
(1) Naive error heuristics fail because over $21\%$ of updates induce negative marginal utility, and inter-splat coupling causes extreme non-additivity;
(2) Unconstrained densification induces compute dilution, whereas Dual Coverage and Backlog Throttling resolves this bottleneck, delivering \textbf{+2.87 dB} mean PSNR gain (paired Cohen's $d_z = 38.42$, Holm-adjusted $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \le 0.0391$, $\eta = 76.4\%$ headroom recovery) with a 3.8$\times$ smaller map;
(3) Fast Approximate Attribution enables 614$\times$ faster attribution ($0.85\,\mathrm{ms}$);
(4) Validation on 3,669 continuous frames demonstrates sustained interactive mapping ($8.7\,\mathrm{FPS}$, $21.08\,\mathrm{dB}$ final PSNR);
(5) Lifetime convergence diagnostics conclusively reject optimization starvation; and
(6) Multi-scene evaluation across TUM RGB-D and Replica confirms scene-dependent advantages with an $\approx 15\times$ speedup over SplaTAM.
